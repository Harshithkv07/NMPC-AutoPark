The Problem

Cars such as the Mahindra BE 6 and XEV 9e can already park themselves (Autopark: 12 ultrasonic sensors, perpendicular, angled and parallel bays). A truck with a trailer cannot, and it is the vehicle that needs help most. The reason is physics: a tractor-trailer is two bodies joined at a hitch, and when it reverses, the hitch angle grows on its own. A 2° error becomes 60° after 28 m of reversing. If nobody corrects it, the trailer folds against the cab: a jackknife.

What We Built

A guidance system that takes a tractor-trailer from the yard gate into a chosen bay while the hitch angle never exceeds 60°. It has four layers:

LayerWhat it doesPresented in
Vehicle modelFour numbers describe the rig; four equations say how they change for a given speed and steeringPart 1 (A), Part 2 (B)
Hybrid A* plannerSearches the yard once and returns a route the truck can actually drive, gate to bayPart 3 (C)
NMPC controllerEvery 0.25 s, predicts 9.6 s ahead and chooses the steering that follows the route with |ψ| ≤ 60° as a hard limitPart 3 (C)
Driver guidanceTurns the controller's output into gear, speed (km/h), steering-wheel turns and distancePart 4 (D)

The Result

We tested 110 free bays (4 layouts × 6 random fills of parked trucks). All 110 were parked within tolerance, the hitch limit was never broken (worst case 50.1°), and a driver model without hitch awareness jackknifed on all 72 reversing manoeuvres.

The Chain of Ideas

THE STORY IN FOUR SENTENCES

(1) We model the truck as a wheeled robot with four state variables and two inputs. (2) Its kinematics show that reversing is unstable: the hitch angle grows exponentially. (3) So we plan a drivable route with Hybrid A* and follow it with an NMPC that looks ahead and treats the hitch limit as a hard constraint. (4) The result parks every test bay and tells a human driver exactly what to do.
